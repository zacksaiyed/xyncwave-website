# AI & Intelligent Automation

Find the workflow where intelligence can prove its usefulness.

AI becomes commercially relevant when it improves a defined task with available information, evaluable output, responsible review, and a reason to move beyond experimentation.

## A model is not a use case, and a demonstration is not an operating result.

Pressure to adopt AI can create isolated pilots that are technically interesting but disconnected from daily work. A credible opportunity starts with a repeated task, the information required to perform it, the people responsible for checking it, and a result that can be evaluated. Only then is it useful to decide whether AI, deterministic automation, better search, improved data, or a simpler process is the right intervention.

## Why AI experiments fail to become useful work

The gap is usually not model capability alone; it is the absence of a controlled operating context.

### Undefined task

The idea is framed as ‘use AI’ rather than a specific retrieval, review, classification, drafting, or decision-support activity.

### Unavailable information

Required knowledge is fragmented, inaccessible, outdated, or too sensitive for the proposed approach.

### No evaluation method

Teams cannot agree what a correct, useful, safe, or sufficiently reliable result looks like.

### Missing human boundary

The workflow does not define who reviews output, handles uncertainty, or owns the eventual decision.

### Disconnected pilot

A prototype sits outside the systems and roles where the actual work occurs.

### Production assumptions

A promising demonstration is treated as proof of security, scale, cost, supportability, or sustained value.

## Unfocused experimentation consumes attention without reducing uncertainty.

Teams accumulate prototypes but still cannot answer whether the use case is feasible, safe, useful, and worth integrating. Expectations rise faster than evidence. A bounded proof should narrow the decision: proceed, change the approach, improve the information foundation, or stop.

## A small proof with a clear decision at the end

The target workflow defines its users, information boundary, expected output, review steps, failure cases, and evaluation criteria. A proof can then test the uncertain parts without being represented as a production result. If useful, the next phase addresses integration, governance, monitoring, cost, security, and operational ownership.

- Name the recurring task
- Inspect information quality and access
- Define useful and unacceptable output
- Set human review and risk boundaries
- Build a bounded proof
- Decide whether and how to operationalize

The following conditions make a focused investigation more credible.

- People search several sources to answer routine questions
- Document review creates a visible queue
- A product feature could benefit from assisted intelligence
- A human can evaluate output consistently
- The necessary information exists and can be governed
- A narrow proof can be isolated from critical decisions
- Leadership needs evidence before funding a larger programme

## Turn AI interest into an evidence-producing sequence

The first engagement should make the decision safer, not make the ambition sound larger.

### Opportunity framing

Define the task, users, volume, current effort, desired output, and reason intelligence may help.

### Information and risk review

Assess sources, quality, permissions, sensitivity, evaluation, and unacceptable failure modes.

### Bounded proof

Build the smallest experience that tests feasibility and usefulness with representative examples.

### Operational decision

Document findings and decide whether to stop, iterate, improve foundations, or engineer a production path.

## Questions that separate a useful AI proof from a technology demo

### Do we need an enterprise AI strategy before starting?

No. A bounded workflow can be explored first, provided information governance and decision ownership are clear.

### How do you identify a worthwhile use case?

Look for recurring cognitive work, accessible information, an evaluable output, meaningful operational burden, and a responsible human review point.

### What does a proof of concept establish?

It can test feasibility, information fit, user usefulness, failure patterns, and the next engineering decision. It does not establish production scale or ROI by itself.

### Could conventional automation be a better answer?

Yes. If rules are stable and explicit, deterministic workflow automation may be more reliable and easier to govern than AI.

### How is sensitive information handled?

The information boundary, permissions, provider behavior, retention, and review requirements must be defined for the specific proof and environment.

### When should an AI idea be stopped?

When the task is poorly defined, information is unsuitable, output cannot be evaluated, risk exceeds the value, or a simpler intervention solves the problem better.
